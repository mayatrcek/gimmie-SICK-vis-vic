# Extensive Pelagic Habitat Use by Adult Yellowtail Kingfish (*Seriola lalandi*) in the Tasman Sea

**Goddard et al., 2026** — *Canadian Journal of Fisheries and Aquatic Sciences*
DOI: [10.1139/cjfas-2025-0343](https://doi.org/10.1139/cjfas-2025-0343)

---

## Aim

- Fill the gap in knowledge about the horizontal, vertical, and thermal habitat preferences of adult Yellowtail Kingfish, a species of high commercial and recreational value.
- Quantify how kingfish use continental shelf ("on-shelf") versus offshore ("off-shelf") waters off south-eastern Australia and New Zealand.

## Methods

- Satellite telemetry used to track movement, depth, and water temperature.
- **50 adult kingfish** tracked over a combined **3,159 days** (median 34 days per fish; sd ± 81.8).
- Individuals travelled up to **4,101 km** (mean 351 km; sd ± 790).

## Key Findings

### Extensive offshore use
- Kingfish moved between on-shelf and off-shelf waters, spending **1,832 days off-shelf** — more than half of total tracking days.
- Challenges the conventional view of kingfish as a mainly coastal/reef-associated fish.

### Mostly shallow, but capable of deep dives
- Vertical distribution largely restricted to the **upper 100 m**.
- Fish tagged near offshore islands tended to use **deeper water**.
- **6 individuals** dove beyond 200 m, with a **maximum recorded depth of 475 m**.

### Broad thermal tolerance
- Occupied a temperature range spanning **16 °C (11.0–27.0 °C)**.
- Showed a clear affinity for **17.4–20.8 °C**.

### Cross-jurisdictional movement
- Individuals crossed between fisheries jurisdictions (Australian states and New Zealand).

## Management Implications

- The diversity of movements — offshore and cross-jurisdictional — reflects complex behaviour and population dynamics.
- Single-jurisdiction management may not match how the population actually moves; broader, coordinated management should be considered.

## Practical Takeaway

The eastern population is not tied to the continental shelf. These fish range widely into open Tasman Sea pelagic habitat, sit mostly in the top 100 m within a ~17–21 °C comfort zone, but will use much deeper, cooler water — especially around offshore islands and seamounts.
